## The idea

Monuments, mosaics, mural paintings, caves and manuscripts are living surfaces. Bacteria, fungi, algae and lichens colonise
them, and a warmer, drier climate with more violent rain episodes changes who grows there and how fast the material degrades.
The talk asks three questions:

1. What do we already know about the microbes living on heritage surfaces, in Tunisia and elsewhere?
2. What can amplicon sequencing, metagenomics and metatranscriptomics tell a conservator that the eye cannot?
3. Can microclimate data, omics and AI be combined to move from curative to preventive conservation?

## Recommendations for people working in this field

- **Start with metadata.** Record material, orientation, temperature, relative humidity, light and visitor pressure before taking any sample. A sequence without its context cannot be compared or modelled.
- **Sample with the conservators, not after them.** Agree on non-invasive sampling with the site authority, and take a baseline before any cleaning or biocide treatment.
- **Go beyond "who is there".** 16S/ITS gives a census. Shotgun metagenomics shows what the community can do, and RNA shows who is active. DNA alone also counts dead cells.
- **Treat stone and plaster as low-biomass samples.** Include extraction and field blanks, and report them.
- **Monitor over seasons.** One sampling campaign is a photograph. Degradation is a film.
- **Deposit the data.** Open reads with standard metadata (ENA/SRA, MIxS) are what make cross-site comparison and AI models possible.
- **Build mixed teams.** Microbiologists, conservators, geologists, climatologists and data scientists each hold one part of the answer.

## Note on the SRA figures

The NCBI SRA counts shown in the slides (datasets, BioProjects and runs per country or site) are **approximate**. They come from keyword searches of the SRA done in November 2026, they depend on the search terms and on the counting unit, and they change as new data are deposited. Please read them as orders of magnitude, not exact values, and rerun the search before citing them.

*Les valeurs SRA présentées dans les diapositives sont approximatives : elles proviennent de recherches par mots-clés dans NCBI SRA (novembre 2026) et doivent être lues comme des ordres de grandeur.*
